Hiring managers in the US often spend less than a minute on a first read of a resume. If yours follows a format they don't expect, it can be set aside before they reach your best experience. These guidelines will help your resume look familiar to a US reader.

## Leave out personal details

In many countries it is normal to include a photo, date of birth, marital status or nationality. In the US, **leave these out**. Employers are careful about anti-discrimination laws, and many prefer not to see this information at all.

Include only:

- Your full name
- City and country (and time zone if you're applying for remote roles)
- Professional email address and phone number with country code
- A link to your LinkedIn profile (and portfolio or GitHub, if relevant)

## Keep it short

One page is ideal for early-career professionals. Two pages is fine with substantial experience. Anything longer is rarely read.

## Lead with results, not duties

Instead of describing what your job was, show what you achieved. Use strong verbs and numbers wherever you can.

- ❌ *Responsible for customer support emails.*
- ✅ *Resolved 60+ customer tickets per day while keeping a 95% satisfaction score.*

## Use a clear, simple layout

- Reverse chronological order — most recent job first.
- Consistent dates in a US-friendly format, such as *Mar 2022 – Present*.
- A standard font, clear headings and no tables or text boxes. Many companies use applicant tracking systems (ATS) that struggle to read complex layouts.
- Save and send it as a PDF unless the employer asks for Word.

## Match the job description

Read the job posting carefully and use the same words it uses for skills and tools — where they truthfully describe your experience. This helps both the ATS and the human reader see the match quickly.

## Explain international experience

US recruiters may not know your previous employers. Add a short line of context:

> *Acme Software — a 200-person SaaS company serving retail clients across Europe.*

If your degree is from outside the US, you can add its US equivalent if you have had it evaluated.

## Check your English carefully

Spelling and grammar mistakes are one of the most common reasons resumes are rejected. Read it aloud, use a spelling checker set to US English, and ask a fluent speaker to review it.

## Finally: be honest

Never exaggerate titles, dates or skills. US employers often run background and reference checks, and a mismatch can end an otherwise successful hiring process.
