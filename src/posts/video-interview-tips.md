For international candidates, the first conversation with a US company almost always happens over Zoom, Google Meet or Microsoft Teams. Technical problems and small misunderstandings can distract from what really matters: your skills and experience. Use this checklist to prepare.

## Before the day

- **Confirm the time zone.** Write the interview time in your local time *and* the interviewer's time zone, and check it carefully. Daylight saving changes in the US and your country may not happen on the same dates.
- **Test your setup.** Use the same app, device and room you'll use on the day. Check your camera, microphone and internet speed.
- **Research the company.** Know what they do, who their customers are, and why the role interests you.
- **Prepare your stories.** US interviewers often ask behavioral questions such as *"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate."*

## Use the STAR method

A simple way to structure answers to behavioral questions:

1. **Situation** — the context, in one or two sentences.
2. **Task** — what you needed to achieve.
3. **Action** — what *you* did (say "I", not only "we").
4. **Result** — the outcome, with numbers if possible.

Prepare four or five STAR stories that show different strengths: solving a problem, leading, learning quickly, handling a mistake.

## On the day

- Join **two or three minutes early**.
- Sit facing a window or a lamp so your face is well lit, and keep the camera at eye level.
- Use headphones with a microphone to avoid echo.
- Close other apps and silence notifications.
- Dress as you would for an in-person interview at that company.

## During the conversation

- **Speak clearly and at a steady pace.** A short pause before you answer is perfectly fine.
- **Ask for clarification** if you didn't understand a question. It's far better than answering the wrong question.
- **Look at the camera** when you make important points — it feels like eye contact to the interviewer.
- **Be ready to talk about remote work.** Explain how you manage your time, communicate progress, and handle time-zone differences.

## Questions to ask them

Always have two or three questions ready, for example:

- *What does success look like in the first 90 days?*
- *How does the team communicate day-to-day?*
- *What hours would you like me to overlap with the team?*

## After the interview

Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours. Mention something specific from the conversation and restate your interest in the role.
