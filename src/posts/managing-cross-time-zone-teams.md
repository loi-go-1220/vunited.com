Hiring internationally gives you access to a much larger pool of talent. It also means your team will not always be online at the same time. Handled well, that can even be an advantage: work moves forward while part of the team is asleep. Here is what works.

## Agree on overlap hours

Decide how many hours each day everyone should be available at the same time. For many teams, **two to four hours of overlap** is enough for meetings, quick questions and handovers. Put these hours in writing so expectations are clear from day one.

## Write things down

When people can't just walk over to a colleague's desk, written communication becomes essential.

- Keep decisions, processes and project status in a shared place everyone can find.
- Write messages that make sense on their own — include the context, the question and the deadline.
- Record important meetings or share short summaries for people who couldn't attend.

## Make meetings count

- Rotate meeting times if the overlap is small, so the same people aren't always joining early or late.
- Share an agenda in advance and end with clear next steps and owners.
- Replace status meetings with written updates where possible.

## Onboard with care

The first few weeks shape how a new team member works with you for years.

- Prepare accounts and tools before their first day.
- Assign a buddy who can answer everyday questions.
- Set clear goals for the first 30, 60 and 90 days.
- Schedule regular one-on-ones, especially early on.

## Respect local calendars

Public holidays, weekends and working norms differ between countries. Keep a shared calendar of holidays across your team and plan deadlines around them.

## Focus on outcomes

Measure people by the quality and reliability of their work, not by when their status light is green. Clear goals and regular feedback build trust in both directions.

## Build connection

Remote teams need intentional social time: a few minutes of casual conversation at the start of meetings, a channel for non-work chat, and celebrating wins together. Small habits add up to a team that feels like a team.
