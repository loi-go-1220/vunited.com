More US companies are looking beyond their local market to fill roles. The reasons are simple: there are skilled people everywhere, and remote work has made it practical to work with them. But "hiring internationally" can mean several different things, and the right approach depends on the role.

Here are the most common options and what to consider for each.

## 1. Hire remotely as an independent contractor

The simplest route for many roles. The professional stays in their home country and works for you as an independent contractor.

- **Good for:** project-based work, specialist skills, and teams testing remote hiring for the first time.
- **Things to consider:** contractor relationships must genuinely be contractor relationships. How much control you have over hours, tools and methods matters. Misclassifying an employee as a contractor can create legal and tax problems in the worker's country.

## 2. Hire through an Employer of Record (EOR)

An Employer of Record is a company that legally employs the worker in their country on your behalf. It handles local payroll, taxes, benefits and employment law, while the person works day-to-day as part of your team.

- **Good for:** long-term, full-time team members abroad, without setting up your own foreign entity.
- **Things to consider:** EOR services charge a fee on top of salary, but they reduce compliance risk significantly.

## 3. Bring talent to the United States

For roles that need someone on-site, US employers can sponsor work visas. Common categories include:

- **H-1B** for specialty occupations that usually require a bachelor's degree or higher. New H-1B visas are subject to an annual cap and a lottery-based selection, so timing matters.
- **TN** for qualifying professionals from Canada and Mexico under the USMCA agreement.
- **L-1** for employees transferring from a company's office abroad.
- **O-1** for individuals with extraordinary ability in their field.

Visa rules change and every case is different, so always work with a qualified US immigration attorney before making an offer that depends on sponsorship.

## How to choose

Ask three questions about the role:

1. **Does the work need to happen in the US?** If not, remote options are faster and simpler.
2. **Is it ongoing or project-based?** Ongoing roles usually suit employment (directly or via an EOR). Defined projects can suit contracts.
3. **How much time do you have?** Remote hires can start in weeks. Visa-based hires can take months.

## Getting the most from international hires

- **Be clear about working hours.** Agree on how many hours overlap with your US team.
- **Invest in onboarding.** Document how your team works, and give new hires a go-to person in their first weeks.
- **Assess communication, not just skills.** Strong written and spoken English makes remote collaboration much easier.
